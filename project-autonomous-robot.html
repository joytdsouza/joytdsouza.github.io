Class Project Completed

Autonomous Competition Robot

ME2110 · Atlanta, GA · Sep 2025 – Nov 2025

Labeled CAD model for the robot we used in the final competition.

Objective

In ME2110: Creative Decisions and Design, groups of 4 work together over 8 weeks to build an autonomous robot with a final bill of materials of $120. Groups are given a mechatronics kit with components such as pneumatic pistons, solenoids, motors, various sensors, and an Arduino Uno. Each team’s robot competes in a final competition, where robots must complete various themed tasks to earn points. These tasks include reaching high objects, manipulating objects to specific positions, and deploying objects at specific times to certain locations. Groups are also responsible for creating technical reports and giving presentations throughout the build process.

Project Description

My team’s robot focused on creating modular subsystems that could easily be interchanged and minimizing human errors and the need for hand tools through utilizing machine manufacturing methods as much as possible. Before each build iteration, the entire robot was modeled in Onshape, where I exported.svgs and .step files for laser cutting and 3D printing (our main methods of fabrication). I spent a lot of time in the IDEALab - the dedicated studio space for 2110- laser-cutting practice squares on MDF boards and queuing my prints on the BambuLab P1S printers. I also spent a lot of time assembling the robot (we bought our own ratchets to speed up assembly). Specific parts of the robot I CADed included mounts to hold one of the mounting boards and to hold the pneumatic piston in the sheep subsystem. I also created two alignment jigs to center the robot on the track.

Reflection

2110 is notorious for being a time-draining class, and for a good reason. The class required persistent effort and constant communication week-over-week. Anything was bound to go wrong at any time (the air tank we were given was broken), and every Thursday, we had deliverables due at 8 AM for our studio section. Despite the challenge, I came out of the course knowing a whole lot more than I did before. From my professors, I learned about design concept generation and evaluation through Function Trees and Morphological Charts, quality function deployment and voice of the consumer with Engineering Specification Sheets and House of Quality, and Design for X (DFM, DFA, etc.). I also implemented these design concepts and tools in technical reports and presentations, practicing my technical communication. From my groupmates, I learned what goes into building a robot that you can’t learn from a lecture, especially when it comes to modeling and fabrication. Whether it was designing rack and pinion and scissor-link mechanisms in our subsystems or buying locknuts and ratchets, I knew each time I worked with my teammates, I would walk away knowing something new. This project also showed me that I have skills as a project manager. Communication was extremely important in order to meet the weekly deliverables. I took on a project management role for our team, setting up weekly team calls to plan for reports and any prototyping that needed to be done. I also organized a file system for the many documents needed to make each report and presentation, streamlining the report writing process.
