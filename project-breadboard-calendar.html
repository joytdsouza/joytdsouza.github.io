Breadboard Calendar

Personal Project · Atlanta, GA · 2025 – Present

Caption goes here — describe what this main image shows.

Objective

I wanted to give one of my friends a personalized and practical gift for their birthday. They had recently switched majors to electrical engineering, which led me to think of a calendar made from electrical components. Upon discovering no such gift existed, I decided to make my own.

Project Description

I imagined a perpetual calendar that displayed the date using electrical components arranged on a breadboard. I set the following criteria for my design: easily fits on a small desk, date must be quickly readable, and parts must be realistic, recognizable electrical components. After researching electrical components, I selected parts based on their geometry to fit on a breadboard and visual elements I could display the date with. Real breadboards have raised numbers, so I replaced these with letters for days of the week. To indicate the current day, I needed a small component the user would move each day, so I picked an LED. Square rectifiers typically have four characters, which I used for day numbers and ordinal indicator (st, nd, etc.). To put month name and number on one component, I used Raspberry Pi Picos, which are long and rectangular, and contain information written on the board.

Reflection

My first print failed because I dimensioned the breadboard too closely to a real breadboard, thus forcing all the other parts to be too small to print properly - some parts were missing details or broke easily when the supports were removed. I wanted to make the final product as realistic as possible, but I didn’t account for tradeoffs that would be needed in terms of fabrication. Realizing that replicating the scale produced bad parts in terms of quality also made me consider that having a small calendar wasn’t conducive to my goal of a practical gift. For my second iteration, I scaled all components by 150%, and the printed parts showed major improvements in quality. I plan to have iteration two printed very soon.
