Claw Attachment

Georgia Tech, Undergraduate Research · Atlanta, GA · Jan 2026 – Present

Caption goes here — describe what this main image shows.

Objective

Working with a fellow GT peer to integrate a Raspberry Pi 5, machine vision system, and custom mechanical gripper with a UR5 robotic arm for industrial pick-and-place applications.

Project Description

On the software side, the model was trained on images from multiple perspectives of differently colored test tubes in various placements within a holding tray. Using RoboFlow, I labeled test tubes in the training images. For hardware, the existing mechanical claw was too large to fit within adjacent test tubes to grab them. After remaking the gripper in Onshape, I modeled new, smaller grippers that were compatible with the gear mechanism inside of the claw. To validate the new grippers would fit between the test tubes before fabricating, I created a working gripper assembly with mates mimicking the gear movement, and tested it with models of the holding tray with test tubes. I printed these attachments from PETG in order to have the durability and strength to work with the metal claw framework. The next steps for this project are adding a tactile surface to the portion of the claw that will grip the tube, and running the model with the new gripper.

Reflection

This project was my first time modeling an existing object in CAD. I decided which gripper components needed to be replaced to modify the claw, and then determine which existing components I needed to remodel in order to make my new parts work within the system. I also learned about different types of 3D printing filaments while trying to find one that would grip test tubes the best. I initially thought of TPU because it seemed more tactile than other materials, but it was also too flexible when I needed a solid claw. Seeking advice from staff at a makerspace on campus, they helped me decide on using PETG rather than PLA, and that adding a tactical material adhesively to the claw would be more effective. In my first iteration, I made the thicknesses too thin, and the print came out poorly, especially around the connection holes for the linkages. On the second try, I looked for parts of the claw where I could increase the thickness without affecting the part of the gripper that picked up the test tubes. Fiddling with the thicknesses was difficult because the bottom part of the gripper had to be less than a certain thickness to fit between the test tubes, so I had to adjust the rest of the gripper without compromising the structure and without shifting the fastener holes.
