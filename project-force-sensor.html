Club Project Ongoing

Force Sensor for 3D-Printed Prosthetic

Medical Robotics Club · Atlanta, GA · Sep 2025 – Present

FSR calibration set up.

Objective

Medical Robotics is a club focused on robotic solutions in the medical field with multiple teams working on different projects. Team Limbo aims to create an affordable, 3D-printed prosthetic for transradial amputees. Within Team Limbo, I’m part of the force sensor subteam, which is integrating Force Sensor Resistors (FSR) into the fingertips to provide haptic feedback.

Project Description

FSRs need to be calibrated in order to accurately report their readings. To do this, we developed a standard operating procedure (SOP) that involves applying varying known weights to the FSR, recording the sensor values, and correlating the two data sets to calibrate the sensor. I helped to design the physical set up for this SOP, which comprises of a level surface to place the FSR on, and a method of concentrating the force from the weight directly on the center of the FSR. FSRs give the most accurate readings in the center of the sensor, so I created a simple frustum-like device. Weights are placed on the large, upper face and the force is channeled into the smaller, bottom face that is sized to fit exactly on the sensor. Calibration will be completed soon, and the next steps are implementing the FSRs into finger.

Reflection

While there is a mechanical subteam in Team Limbo, I chose to work on the electrical subteam with the FSR because I get a lot of experience with designing in CAD, being a mechanical engineering major, but not as much with the electrical side. However, hardware and software often work together in a build, and learning about design from an electrical perspective will allow me to have a more full understanding when I work on mechanical design. I am also excited by the prospect of delivering a potentially life-changing product to someone who needs it in the future.
